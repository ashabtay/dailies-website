How to turn on notifications for an app on iPhone

An app can only send you notifications if you said yes, and the moment it asked was probably the first time you opened it, when you were tapping through as fast as you could. Here is how to turn them on afterwards, how to choose where they show up, and what to do when the app is not in the list at all.

The steps

Every app's notifications are switched on and off in one place, the Settings app:

  1. Open Settings and tap Notifications. It sits in the same group as Sounds & Haptics, Focus and Screen Time.
  2. Find the app. The list under Notification Style has every app that has asked to send notifications, in alphabetical order. Tap the one you want.
  3. Turn on Allow Notifications. The switch at the top. Everything under it only appears once it is on.
  4. Choose where they appear. Tick Lock Screen, Notification Center and Banners. Leave all three on unless you have a reason not to.
  5. Pick a banner style. Temporary banners slide away by themselves. Persistent ones stay at the top of the screen until you deal with them.
  6. Set sounds and badges. Turn on Sounds if you want to hear them, and Badges for the red count on the app's icon.

The same page is also inside each app's own entry in Settings: scroll down the main Settings list to the app's name, tap it, then tap Notifications. Both lead to the same switches.

Where they show up

The three places under Alerts are worth understanding, because turning one off by accident is the most common reason a notification seems to vanish:

  • Lock Screen. The notification waits on the Lock Screen until you unlock the phone or clear it.
  • Notification Center. The list you see when you swipe down from the top of the screen. Older notifications collect here.
  • Banners. The card that slides in at the top while you are using the phone.

If an app's notifications only ever appear in the list and never pop up, Banners is off. If they never reach the Lock Screen, the Lock Screen box is unticked.

How notifications are laid out on the Lock Screen is a separate setting for all apps at once: Settings, Notifications, Display As. List shows them in full, Stack piles them up, and Count shows only a number until you tap it.

The app is not in the list

An app only appears under Notifications once it has asked for permission. If it is missing, it has not asked yet. Open the app and use it for a moment: most apps ask the first time you open them, or the first time you turn on something that needs notifications, such as a reminder.

When the question appears, tap Allow. If you tapped Don't Allow at some point, the app cannot ask again, and the only way back is the switch in Settings from the steps above.

On, but nothing arrives

If the switch is on and nothing comes, something else is holding them back. The usual suspects, in the order worth checking:

  • A Focus. Do Not Disturb, Sleep or Work can silence an app that is not on its allowed list. Open Control Center and see whether a Focus is on.
  • Scheduled Summary. If the app is in your summary, its notifications are held back and delivered in a batch at the times you chose.
  • Low Power Mode and Background App Refresh, which can delay some apps.

Each of these, with its fix, is in not getting notifications from an app on iPhone.

Questions

How do I turn on notifications for an app on my iPhone?

Open Settings, tap Notifications, tap the app and turn on Allow Notifications. Then make sure Lock Screen, Notification Center and Banners are ticked.

Why is an app not showing in my notification settings?

The app has not asked for permission yet. Open it and use it for a moment; most apps ask the first time you open them or turn on a feature that needs notifications. Once it has asked, it appears in the list.

I tapped Don't Allow. Can I change my mind?

Yes. The app cannot ask a second time, but you can turn its notifications on yourself in Settings, Notifications, then the app, with the Allow Notifications switch.

How do I make notifications stay on the screen?

In the app's notification settings, set Banner Style to Persistent. The banner then stays at the top of the screen until you tap it or swipe it away.

Notifications are on but I still get nothing. Why?

A Focus such as Do Not Disturb or Sleep may be silencing the app, or the app may be in your Scheduled Summary. Check Control Center for a Focus, then Settings, Notifications, Scheduled Summary.

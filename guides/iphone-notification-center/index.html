iPhone Notification Center vs Lock Screen vs Banners: what is the difference?

A notification on your iPhone can show up in three places: as a banner at the top of the screen, on the Lock Screen, and in Notification Center. They are not three kinds of notification. They are three places the same one can appear, and you choose which of them each app uses. Here is what each one is, how to open and clear Notification Center, and where the choice lives in Settings.

The difference in one table

The short version: a banner is the moment a notification arrives, the Lock Screen is where it waits while the phone is locked, and Notification Center is where it is kept until you clear it.

One notification can use all three. It slides in as a banner if you are using the phone, or lands on the Lock Screen if you are not, and either way it is then kept in Notification Center until you clear it. Whether a banner goes away by itself or waits for you is a separate setting, Banner Style, with a guide of its own: iPhone notification banner style: Temporary or Persistent?

The Lock Screen

The Lock Screen is what you see when you raise or tap a locked iPhone: the clock, your wallpaper and widgets, and below them the notifications that came in since you last unlocked it, grouped by app with the newest on top.

From here you can tap a notification to open it (the phone asks for Face ID or your passcode first), swipe left on it to clear it, or swipe up from the middle of the screen to see everything in Notification Center.

Banners

A banner is the card that slides in at the top of the screen when a notification arrives while you are using the phone, so you see it without leaving what you are doing. Tap it to open the app, or swipe it up to put it away.

A banner is only the arrival. Once it goes, the notification is in Notification Center, as long as that box is ticked for the app. How long the banner itself stays is Banner Style: Temporary lets it go after a few seconds, Persistent keeps it until you deal with it. Both, and which to pick, are in the banner style guide.

Notification Center

Notification Center is the list of every notification you have not cleared yet, the history behind the Lock Screen and the banners. Every iPhone has it, and there is nothing to turn on. To open it and tidy it up:

  1. Open Notification Center. While you are using the phone, swipe down from the top left corner of the screen. On the Lock Screen, swipe up from the middle of the screen.
  2. Scroll up to see older notifications. Anything you have not cleared is still in the list, grouped by app.
  3. Tap a group to open it. Its notifications spread out one by one. Tap Show Less to fold them back.
  4. Clear one notification or one group. Swipe left on it, then tap Clear or Clear All.
  5. Clear everything at once. Tap the small x button, which Apple calls Clear Notifications, then tap Clear.
  6. Close it. Swipe up from the bottom of the screen with one finger.

A cleared notification is gone for good, so clear only what you are done with. If Notification Center will not open from the Lock Screen, open Settings, tap Face ID & Passcode, and turn on Notification Center under Allow Access When Locked.

Why notifications leave the Lock Screen after you unlock

This is the part that catches most people out. Notifications pile up on the Lock Screen while the phone is locked, you unlock it, and the next time you look at the Lock Screen they are gone. Nothing was deleted. Once you have unlocked the phone, the notifications count as seen and move off the Lock Screen to make room for new ones. They stay in Notification Center until you clear them.

To get them back, swipe up from the middle of the Lock Screen, or swipe down from the top left corner when the phone is unlocked, and scroll up. If an app's notifications are never in the list at all, its Notification Center box is unticked, which is the next section.

Choose where an app's notifications go

Each app has its own three boxes, one for each place, so one app can reach you everywhere while another stays in the list:

  1. Open Settings and tap Notifications. The apps that have asked to send notifications are listed under Notification Style.
  2. Tap the app. Allow Notifications at the top has to be on, or nothing else on the page shows.
  3. Under Alerts, tick the places you want. Lock Screen, Notification Center and Banners sit side by side. A tick means the app's notifications show there. The change is saved straight away.

Some mixes that work well:

  • All three ticked. You see it arrive, it waits on the Lock Screen, and it is kept in the list. Right for the apps you never want to miss.
  • Lock Screen and Notification Center, no Banners. Nothing slides over what you are doing, and the notification waits quietly for the next time you look.
  • Notification Center only. The app stays out of sight until you go looking. Good for things you check when you have time.

Display As and Show Previews

Two settings at the top of Settings, Notifications change how the Lock Screen shows what lands on it:

  • Display As sets the layout for every app. Count shows only a number at the bottom of the screen until you tap it, Stack piles the notifications up with the newest on top, and List shows each one in full. Pinching the notifications on the Lock Screen switches between them too.
  • Show Previews decides whether the words of a notification show. Always shows them, When Unlocked hides them until the phone recognizes you, and Never shows only the app's name. An app can have its own choice on its page, under Lock Screen Appearance.

Scheduled Summary and Focus

Scheduled Summary holds back the notifications of the apps you pick and delivers them together at the times you set, instead of one by one as they come. Turn it on in Settings, Notifications, Scheduled Summary, then choose the times and the apps. While it is on, each app's page has Notification Delivery, where Immediate Delivery takes that app back out.

Focus silences notifications from the apps and people you have not allowed while it is on. They do not light up the screen, play a sound or show a banner, and they wait in Notification Center for later. A Focus can bring its own Lock Screen with it: how to link a Lock Screen to a Focus on iPhone.

For Dailies

Dailies sends quotes as notifications inside the hours you choose, at moments you did not pick. The three boxes decide where each line finds you:

  • Banners shows a quote the moment it comes, if you are using the phone.
  • Lock Screen leaves it waiting for the next time you pick the phone up.
  • Notification Center keeps the day's quotes in the list, so you can scroll back through them in the evening.

To never miss one, tick all three. To be interrupted less, untick Banners for Dailies and keep the other two: the lines wait quietly, and a swipe up from the middle of the Lock Screen brings them all back. If you added Dailies to Scheduled Summary, its quotes arrive bundled together instead of at their own moments, and setting its Notification Delivery to Immediate Delivery undoes that.

Questions

What is the difference between the Lock Screen, Notification Center and banners on iPhone?

They are three places the same notification can show. A banner slides in at the top of the screen when a notification arrives while you are using the phone. The Lock Screen shows the notifications that came in while the phone was locked. Notification Center is the list that keeps all of them until you clear them. You choose which of the three each app uses in Settings, Notifications, the app, under Alerts.

How do I open Notification Center on iPhone?

Swipe down from the top left corner of the screen while the phone is unlocked. On the Lock Screen, swipe up from the middle of the screen. To close it, swipe up from the bottom of the screen.

How do I see old notifications on iPhone?

Open Notification Center and scroll up. Every notification you have not cleared is there, grouped by app. Notifications you have cleared are gone and cannot be brought back.

Why do notifications disappear from my Lock Screen after I unlock my iPhone?

Once you unlock the phone, they count as seen and move off the Lock Screen. They are not deleted: swipe up from the middle of the Lock Screen to find them in Notification Center.

How do I clear all notifications on iPhone at once?

Open Notification Center, tap the small x button (Clear Notifications), then tap Clear. To clear just one app's notifications, swipe left on its group and tap Clear All.

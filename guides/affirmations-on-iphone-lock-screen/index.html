How to put affirmations on your iPhone Lock Screen

An affirmation does its work by being read again and again, and the Lock Screen is the one screen you read dozens of times a day without trying. There are three ways to put one there. Which suits you depends on whether you want the same line every time or a few that take turns.

Your own lines in a widget

The most personal version is your own words, which is why the research on affirmations leans toward lines you actually believe over slogans somebody else wrote. A widget lets you have several that take turns, so none of them goes invisible:

  1. Write your lines in the app. In Dailies, open Personalize, then My quotes, and tap Create quote. Write one affirmation per quote. Short ones fit the Lock Screen best.
  2. Follow My quotes. On the Topics screen, follow My quotes. Follow it on its own and the app serves your lines and nothing else. This part needs Premium.
  3. Unlock the iPhone and touch and hold the Lock Screen. Stay on the Lock Screen rather than swiping up, then tap Customize and the Lock Screen preview.
  4. Add the widget. Tap the space under the time, find Dailies in the list and add the wide widget.
  5. Tap Done. Your lines now take turns under the clock, a new one about once an hour.

Your lines stay under My quotes whatever plan you are on, and nobody else sees them. To mix them in with quotes from other people instead, follow My quotes alongside your other topics: your lines turn up among the rest.

An affirmation app's widget

If you would rather not write anything, an affirmation app hands you ready-made lines. The biggest is I am, and it has a Lock Screen widget: add it the same way, from the list under the time. Its lines are written in the first person, to be read as if you were saying them about yourself. Dailies vs I am explains how that differs from reading a quote, and who each one suits.

As a wallpaper

For one line you want in front of you all the time, save it as a picture and make it the wallpaper. Write it in any app that makes images, or save a quote card from Dailies, then set it from the Lock Screen editor with the photo button. Keep the words in the lower half so the clock does not sit on them. The full steps are in how to make a quote wallpaper on iPhone.

The catch is the same as with any wallpaper: after a couple of weeks you stop reading it. Change it when you notice that happening.

Writing ones you will believe

An affirmation that feels false can make a low day worse: when Joanne Wood and colleagues asked people to repeat "I'm a lovable person", the ones with low self-esteem came out feeling worse, not better. The lines that hold up are ones your mind will not argue with:

  • Say what you are doing, not what you are. "I am learning to rest" is easier to believe than "I am calm".
  • Point at evidence. "I have done hard things before" is simply true.
  • Make it about today. "Today I only need to do the next thing" asks less than a promise about your whole life.
  • Borrow a line. If writing about yourself feels awkward, somebody else's sentence can do the same job without asking you to claim anything.

Questions

How do I put affirmations on my iPhone Lock Screen?

Add a widget that shows them to the row under the clock, or save an affirmation as a picture and set it as your wallpaper. A widget can rotate several lines; a wallpaper shows the same one until you change it.

Can I put my own affirmations on my Lock Screen?

Yes. In Dailies, write them under Personalize, My quotes, follow My quotes on the Topics screen with Premium, then add the Dailies widget under the clock. Your own lines take turns there.

Is there a widget for affirmations on iPhone?

Several apps have one, including I am, which writes the affirmations for you. Dailies shows quotes, and with Premium your own lines too, in a Lock Screen widget under the clock.

How do I make an affirmation wallpaper?

Make the affirmation into a picture, in any app that makes images or by saving a quote card, then touch and hold the Lock Screen, tap Customize, and pick the picture with the photo button. Keep the words below the clock.

Do affirmations on your Lock Screen work?

They work best when you believe them. Research by Joanne Wood found that strong positive statements can backfire for people who feel low, so modest, specific lines tend to help more than big claims.

Setting up a new iPhone: Lock Screen, widgets and Focus

Moving your apps and photos to a new iPhone takes care of itself. What does not is everything that made the old one feel like yours: the Lock Screen, the widgets, which apps are allowed to interrupt you. A new phone is the one moment you get to set all of that up from scratch, on purpose. Here it is in order.

The half hour, in order

Do the transfer first, with Quick Start or from your iCloud backup, and let it finish. Then:

  1. Open every app you use daily, once. Widgets and notifications only work for apps that have been opened on this phone. A minute now saves blank widgets later.
  2. Make a Lock Screen you like. Touch and hold the Lock Screen, tap +, and pick a wallpaper, a clock and a widget or two.
  3. Put widgets on the Home Screen. Touch and hold an empty spot, tap Edit, then Add Widget. Put the ones you check most on the first page.
  4. Go through notifications. In Settings, Notifications, turn off everything that does not deserve to interrupt you, and turn on the few that do.
  5. Set up Sleep and Work. In Settings, Focus, choose who and what can reach you in each, and give each its own Lock Screen.
  6. Check Appearance. In Settings, Appearance, choose light or dark and set the Liquid Glass slider from clear to tinted.

Why open your apps first

A new iPhone restores your apps, but until each one has been opened on this phone, iOS treats it as asleep. Its widgets can come back blank, it is missing from the widget list, and it has not yet asked to send you notifications. Open the ones you use every day, say yes to the notifications you want, and close them again. If a widget still shows nothing afterwards, iPhone widget not updating has the fixes.

The Lock Screen

You will look at it more than any other screen, so it is worth getting right. On iOS 27 you can shrink the clock to the size of the date, have Apple Intelligence extend a photo that does not fit, or make a wallpaper from a sentence. iPhone Lock Screen ideas for iOS 27 has eight setups worth copying.

Something that changes by itself keeps the Lock Screen from going invisible after a week. A widget with a short quote, such as Dailies, under the clock is an easy one: a new line from the topics you follow about once an hour.

Home Screen widgets

The Home Screen is where widgets get room. A calendar, the weather and one thing you would like to see more often make a good first page. Stack widgets of the same size on top of each other to keep the page tidy, and swipe through the stack when you want the one underneath. The steps are in how to add a widget to your Home Screen.

Notifications that earn their place

A transfer brings every app's notification setting with it, including the ones you agreed to years ago and never thought about again. Go down the list in Settings, Notifications and ask of each one whether you would miss it. Apps you want to hear from, but not right away, can go in Scheduled Summary, which holds their notifications and delivers them together at the times you pick.

If an app you do want is missing from the list, or stays silent, how to turn on notifications for an app on iPhone covers it.

A Focus for sleep and for work

A Focus decides who and what can reach you, and can switch your Lock Screen and Home Screen with it. Two are worth setting up on day one. Sleep, with a dark Lock Screen and only the people who could need you at night allowed through. Work, with your calendar on the Lock Screen and the apps that are not work kept quiet. How to link a Lock Screen to a Focus walks through both.

Questions

What should I do first on a new iPhone?

Let the transfer from your old iPhone or iCloud backup finish, then open each app you use daily once, so its widgets and notifications start working. After that, set up your Lock Screen, Home Screen widgets, notifications and a Focus for sleep.

Why are my widgets blank on my new iPhone?

An app's widget often stays blank until the app has been opened on the new phone. Open the app once, then go back to the widget. If it is still blank, remove the widget and add it again.

Do my Lock Screens move to a new iPhone?

They come across with a backup or Quick Start, but widgets on them may be empty until their apps are opened on the new phone. It is also a good moment to make a new one.

How do I stop so many notifications on a new iPhone?

Go through Settings, Notifications and turn off the apps you would not miss. Put the ones that can wait in Scheduled Summary, and set up a Sleep Focus so only the people who could need you at night get through.

Where is the Liquid Glass setting?

On iOS 27 it is in Settings, Appearance, Liquid Glass. The slider runs from clear to tinted; tinted is easier to read over busy wallpapers.

Quotes about ego: lines on quiet confidence

Pride, vanity and humility, in the words of people who thought about them for a long time. Grouped by when you'll want them: before you speak, after you've done something good, and when you notice yourself wanting the applause.

By Amit Shabtay, who makes Dailies Published 12 November 2026

Confidence that doesn't need an audience

The old writers didn't use the word ego the way we do. They wrote about pride, vanity and humility, and almost all of them arrived at the same place: the steadiest confidence is the quiet kind.

Humility was never about pretending to be less than you are.

Be modest without diffidence, proud without presumption.

Johann Wolfgang von Goethe

Therefore, the Master knows herself but is not arrogant. She loves herself but also loves others. This is how she is able to make appropriate choices.

Lao Tzu, Tao Te Ching

Self-trust is the first secret of success.

Ralph Waldo Emerson

Those who know others are intelligent; those who know themselves are truly wise.

Lao Tzu, Tao Te Ching

Let the work speak

The ego wants the credit. The work only wants doing. These lines put the attention back where it does some good.

Look at what needs to be done, not at the credit you'll get for doing it.

Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

No need to announce that you will be fair with someone. It will show in what you do.

Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

Praise without making a show of it. Know a lot without showing it off.

Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

When you have accomplished your goal simply walk away.

Lao Tzu, Tao Te Ching

Because she does not like to compete, no one can compete with the things she accomplishes.

Lao Tzu, Tao Te Ching

Do what is needed now. Get moving if you can, and don't look around to see who's watching.

Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

Humility as a strength

Humility is the solid foundation of all the virtues.

Confucius

There are three jewels that I cherish: compassion, moderation, and humility.

Lao Tzu, Tao Te Ching

The soft and pliable overcomes the hard and inflexible.

Lao Tzu, Tao Te Ching

Water is Lao Tzu's favorite picture of this. It takes the lowest place, gives way to everything, and wears down stone.

Nothing is more simple than greatness; indeed, to be simple is to be great.

Ralph Waldo Emerson

Make simplicity and modesty your finest clothes. And love people.

Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

Free of what people think

Much of what we call ego is really a need for approval. Marcus Aurelius, who as emperor had more of it on offer than anyone alive, wrote these notes to himself.

It's odd how we ignore praise from the people beside us and crave it from people we'll never meet.

Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

Why want approval from someone who can't even please themselves?

Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

Make this present moment your own. What people say about you later is nothing to you.

Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

You can be a truly good person and have no one notice. Hold on to that.

Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

Let go of how you'll come across. Be content to live the rest of your life the way your nature asks.

Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

Proud of being humble

The humblebrag isn't new. An emperor spotted it nearly two thousand years ago.

Being proud of how humble you are is the hardest pride of all to bear.

Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

Some people are proud of their humility.

Henry Ward Beecher

Questions

What is a good quote about ego?

Marcus Aurelius's "Carry your confidence quietly: not on display, but not hidden either" says it in one line. For humility as a strength, Lao Tzu's "The soft and pliable overcomes the hard and inflexible" is hard to beat.

Who said "Humility is the solid foundation of all the virtues"?

Confucius, the Chinese teacher and philosopher. The line is credited to him in the classic quotation dictionaries.

What is the difference between ego and confidence?

Confidence is trusting what you can do. Ego is needing other people to see it. The writers here keep the first and let go of the second: do the work well, and leave the applause to take care of itself.

Are the Marcus Aurelius quotes real?

Yes. They come from George Long's 1862 translation of the Meditations, put into plain modern English with the meaning kept. Long's full text is free on Project Gutenberg.

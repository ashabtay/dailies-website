Quotes about discipline and consistency

Discipline quotes online usually come with a photo of someone doing pull-ups at dawn. Real discipline is less cinematic. It's the same small thing, done again, on a day you'd rather not. These lines are about that version.

What discipline actually is

Self-discipline is the ability to get yourself to take action regardless of your emotional state.

Steve Pavlina, Self-Discipline

The key word is regardless. Not "when you're fired up," and not "after you've fought off the urge to skip." Discipline doesn't wait for the right mood, and it doesn't need the wrong one either.

Those who master others are strong; those who master themselves have true power.

Lao Tzu, Tao Te Ching

Said will be a little ahead, but Done should follow at his heel.

Charles Spurgeon

A Victorian preacher's way of saying the gap between talking and doing should be short. It's a nice image: Said and Done as two people on a walk, one of them always trying to keep up.

Success comes from doing, not declaring.

Derek Sivers, sive.rs

Small and often

Nearly every serious writer on habits lands in the same place: consistency beats intensity. A small thing done daily beats a big thing done when you feel like it.

Consistency before intensity. Start small and become the kind of person who shows up every day. Build a new identity. Then increase the intensity.

James Clear, 3-2-1 Newsletter

It's easier to keep a small thing going than a big one. And keeping it going is what matters.

Leo Babauta, Zen Habits

Think not lightly of goodness, that it won’t come back to you. The pot is filled with water falling drop by drop; the attentive one is filled with goodness piled up bit by bit.

Buddha, Dhammapada

The road which looks so long when viewed in its entirety is easily traveled, considered as a series of particular steps.

John Dewey, The Child and the Curriculum

The tallest tree begins as a tiny sprout. The tallest building starts with one shovel of dirt.

Lao Tzu, Tao Te Ching

On habits

It's usually read as a warning about bad habits, but it cuts both ways. The same daily thread that traps you can also hold you up.

Whenever there is a gap between your habits and your goals, your habits will always win.

James Clear, 3-2-1 Newsletter

You're defined by what you do repeatedly. Your habits create your character.

Derek Sivers, How to Live

The trajectory of your life bends in the direction of your habits.

James Clear, 3-2-1 Newsletter

On keeping going

Falling in love is easy. Staying in love is harder. Enthusiasm is common. Endurance is rare.

Derek Sivers, How to Live

Great works are performed, not by strength, but by perseverance.

Samuel Johnson

Johnson spent about nine years on his Dictionary of the English Language, with only a handful of clerks to help. He knew something about perseverance.

Just as it takes muscle to build muscle, it takes self-discipline to build self-discipline.

Steve Pavlina, Self-Discipline

Grow fast, built to crash. Grow slow, built to last.

James Clear, 3-2-1 Newsletter

Making it stick

A quote about discipline is a strange thing to rely on, because the whole point of discipline is that it works on the days you don't feel inspired. But a well-timed line can remind you what you're doing and why, which is half the battle on a flat Wednesday.

Questions

What is the best quote about discipline?

Steve Pavlina's definition is hard to improve on: "Self-discipline is the ability to get yourself to take action regardless of your emotional state." For consistency, James Clear's "Consistency before intensity" is a practical rule to live by.

Who said "Habit is a cable"?

It's usually credited to Horace Mann, the American education reformer: "Habit is a cable. We weave a thread of it every day, and at last we cannot break it."

Is discipline more important than motivation?

For long-term goals, usually yes, because motivation comes and goes. But motivation is useful for choosing goals and getting started. Our article on motivation vs discipline covers how they work together.

How do I become more consistent?

Make the habit small enough to do on your worst day, tie it to something you already do every day, track it somewhere visible, and never let one missed day turn into two.

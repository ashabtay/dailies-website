Quotes for starting over after losing your job

Losing a job takes more than a paycheck. It takes the shape of your days, the people you saw and part of how you answer "what do you do?" These quotes are for the weeks after, whether it was a layoff, a company closing or work that was handed to software and AI.

By Amit Shabtay, who makes Dailies Published 26 November 2026

For the first few days

The first days are often numb, then loud. You don't have to make sense of it yet.

Every so often everything is lost, only to be won back again.

Baltasar Gracián, El Criticón

Just when everything seems to be ending, it begins again.

Baltasar Gracián, El Criticón

Both from Baltasar Gracián, a Spanish writer of the 1600s who had a lot to say about luck turning, in both directions.

Few misfortunes are beyond remedy. Despair lies to us more than hope does.

Vauvenargues, Reflections and Maxims

Despair talks as if it knows how this ends. It doesn't.

For remembering a job isn't who you are

"So, what do you do?" gets harder to answer for a while. It helps to remember what didn't leave with the job.

Fortune can make us poor, sick or grieving, but not wicked, cowardly or defeated. It cannot take away our honesty, our courage, our goodness.

Pierre Charron, Of Wisdom

Written in 1601, and it still sorts things into the two piles that matter: what can be taken from you, and what can't.

I carry no treasures inside me; I only have the power to turn much of what I touch into something of value.

Christian Morgenstern, Stages

That power is what an employer was paying for. It's still yours.

In one life you can rebuild ten fortunes, but with ten fortunes you cannot start a life over.

José Ingenieros, Moral Forces

If your work went to a machine

Some jobs now end because the work is handed to software or AI. That can feel more personal than an ordinary layoff, as if you had been measured against a machine and come up short. You weren't measured. A cost was.

Only one kind of work is blind and mindless: a machine's. Wherever a human arm is working, a mind is at work too.

Manuel González Prada, Hours of Struggle

Manuel González Prada, a Peruvian writer, wrote this more than a century ago, long before anyone worried about AI. The judgment, care and sense you brought to the work were never the machine's part.

Afraid of change? Nothing useful happens without it. Even a warm bath needs the wood to change. You need it too.

Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

Marcus Aurelius on change, with a warm bath for an example. Not comforting, exactly, but steadying.

Whatever the career, the best school will always be learning on the job.

Remy de Gourmont, Dissociations

If what comes next means learning something new, you'll learn most of it the way everyone does: by doing it.

For the job search

Applications, silence, the odd interview, more silence. This part is mostly about keeping going.

Even if your hopes have been let down not seven times but seventy times seven, never lose hope.

Félicité de Lamennais, Words of a Believer

For the rejection emails, and for the replies that never come.

However steep the climb, do not lose heart. Keep your eyes on the reward at the end.

Baltasar Gracián, El Criticón

Work can be a great tonic for a spirit worn down by a long run of defeats.

Concepción Arenal, Penitentiary Studies

Concepción Arenal was a Spanish writer and reformer. Her point holds during a search too: a small project, some volunteering or a few hours of paid work can bring back some of the steadiness.

For starting again

José Enrique Rodó was a Uruguayan essayist, and this is the shortest version of his long book about how people change. Hope isn't the reward at the end. It's the first tool.

Thank God! When the storm is over, the obstacle is gone, and the field is clear to begin again.

Ernest Renan, New Intimate Letters

Ah, to be born again and walk the road, with the lost path found once more!

Antonio Machado, Solitudes, Galleries and Other Poems

The weeks after, practically

In 1982 the social psychologist Marie Jahoda set out why losing work hurts beyond the money. Besides pay, she argued, a job quietly gives you five things: a structure to the day, regular activity, contact with people outside your family, a sense of shared purpose, and a status or identity. Lose the job and all five go at once, which is why the first weeks can feel so strange even when money isn't the worst of it.

Her list doubles as a way to rebuild.

Give the day a shape

Keep the same wake-up time. Put the search in a set block, then stop. A walk at the same hour each day does more than it sounds like it should.

Keep seeing people

Former colleagues, friends, a class, a volunteer shift. The point isn't networking. It's not spending every day alone with the question.

Have something with a purpose

A project with an end, for you or for someone else. Something you can finish while the bigger thing is still open.

Answer the question your own way

"I'm between roles" and "I'm working out what's next" are complete answers.

Dailies has topics for this stretch, including You've done harder things, Decide what's next and Know what you bring. It keeps the lines coming quietly in the background, without asking anything of you.

If it's more than a hard stretch

Quotes can keep you company. They aren't a treatment, and nobody should have to get through a crisis with a list of them. If things have felt dark for weeks, or you're struggling to function, please talk to a doctor, a therapist or someone you trust.

If you're thinking about ending your life or hurting yourself, reach out right now. In the US, call or text 988 for the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. Elsewhere, call your local emergency number.

Questions

What is a good quote for starting over after losing a job?

José Enrique Rodó's "The first tool for starting over is the hope that you can" is short and true. For something about luck turning, Baltasar Gracián wrote: "Just when everything seems to be ending, it begins again."

What can I say to someone who just lost their job?

Something short that doesn't try to fix it: "I'm sorry, that's rotten. I'm here if you want to talk, or not talk." If you want to send a line, Pierre Charron's "Fortune can make us poor, sick or grieving, but not wicked, cowardly or defeated" says what they may need to hear.

How do I stay motivated after losing my job?

Rebuild what the job used to give you besides pay. The psychologist Marie Jahoda listed five things: a structure to the day, regular activity, contact with people, a sense of purpose and a sense of identity. A set routine, people you see each week and one project with an end cover most of them.

What if my job was replaced by AI?

It can feel personal, but it was a decision about costs, not a verdict on you. Manuel González Prada wrote, long before AI: "Only one kind of work is blind and mindless: a machine's. Wherever a human arm is working, a mind is at work too."

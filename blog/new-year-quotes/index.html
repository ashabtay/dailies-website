New Year quotes, and a better way to start the year

Before the plans and resolutions, there's a better place to begin: the year you've just had. Here's a gentle way into January, with quotes for looking back, looking ahead, and sending to people you love.

By Amit Shabtay, who makes Dailies Published 13 November 2026

On this page

Look back before you look ahead

Most New Year advice starts with what to change. Start with what went right instead. In a study published in the Journal of Personality and Social Psychology in 2003, Robert Emmons and Michael McCullough asked people to write down, once a week, a few things they were grateful for. Compared with people who wrote about hassles or ordinary events, they felt better about their lives as a whole, were more hopeful about the week ahead, and even exercised more.

So in the last week of December, before any plans, answer three questions on paper:

  • What went well this year, even a little?
  • What did I learn, or get better at?
  • Who helped me, and do they know?

The third one often ends with a message sent. That's a good way to end a year.

Look back at what has happened to learn from it, not to torment yourself.

Diego de Saavedra Fajardo, The Idea of a Christian Political Prince

Look at the past not as a gloomy memento mori but as a friendly forget-me-not, whose lesson we should remember with love.

Ludwig Börne, Aphorisms and Miscellanies

Months and years go by and are gone forever, but one beautiful moment shines through a whole life.

Franz Grillparzer, Epigrams

Picture the year going well

Then turn round. In 2001 the psychologist Laura King asked students to spend twenty minutes, four days in a row, writing about their best possible future self: life a little way ahead, with things having gone as well as they reasonably could. Published in Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, the study found the writing lifted their mood, and they made fewer visits to the health center in the months after.

Try one page on New Year's Day. Not a list of goals, a description: what a good ordinary Tuesday looks like at the end of next year. The goals tend to show up on their own, and they're usually smaller and kinder than the ones made on January 1.

Choose one line to carry

Resolutions are easy to forget by February. A single sentence is easier to keep. Pick one line that sums up how you'd like the year to feel, and put it somewhere you'll see it every day: the Lock Screen, the fridge, the first page of a notebook. When a choice comes up, it's there to ask.

We are always beginning to live again.

Montaigne, Essays

Nothing is ever done with; a little happiness is enough to make everything begin again.

Émile Zola, Germinal

Every limit is a beginning as well as an ending.

George Eliot, Middlemarch

No! Life has not disappointed me! Year by year I find it truer, more desirable and more mysterious.

Friedrich Nietzsche, The Gay Science

New Year quotes to send

A line from someone else says it better than "Happy New Year" on its own. These are short enough for a message.

The year is a crown woven of flowers, ears of wheat, fruit and dry grass.

Joseph Joubert, Thoughts, Essays and Maxims

We grow fond of the people who saw us happy. We are grateful they were there while we were glad.

Victor Hugo, The Man Who Laughs

Let us be grateful to the people who make us happy. They are the charming gardeners who make our souls blossom.

Marcel Proust, Pleasures and Days

Rest and renewal, a fresh start and hope: a day has ended, long live tomorrow!

Henri-Frédéric Amiel, Amiel's Journal

The last fire of the year invites us to silence, to idleness, to tender rest.

Colette, The Tendrils of the Vine

Questions

What is a good quote for the New Year?

Christian Morgenstern's "Bells at New Year: like the mighty heartbeat of a strong, invincible hope for life" is hard to beat. For something shorter, Montaigne's "We are always beginning to live again" fits on any card.

How should I start the new year?

Look back before you look ahead: write down what went well, what you learned and who helped. Then write one page describing the year going well, and pick one line to carry with you.

What should I write in a New Year message?

Something personal and short. Thank them for something specific from the year, and add a line you like, such as Victor Hugo's "We grow fond of the people who saw us happy."

Do New Year's resolutions work?

More often than the jokes suggest. Our post on New Year's resolutions covers the research, including a 2002 study in which resolvers were far more likely to succeed than people who only meant to change.

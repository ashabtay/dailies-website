1. What a cookie is, briefly

A cookie is a small text file a site asks your browser to keep, so it can recognize the same browser later. Related technologies do the same job by other means — local storage, pixels (a tiny invisible image or script that reports back when a page loads), and device fingerprinting.

This policy covers all of them, not only cookies in the strict sense. Where something is stored in local storage rather than as a cookie, the table says so, because the practical difference matters: clearing cookies does not always clear local storage.

A first-party item is set by mydailies.app itself. A third-party item is set by another company whose code is running on the page. Third-party items are the ones that can follow you between different websites.

7. Changing your mind

Click Cookie settings in the footer of any page. The panel reopens with your current choices and you can change either switch.

Your answer is remembered for one year, after which you are asked again. You will also be asked afresh if these categories ever materially change.

A limitation: withdrawing consent stops the tags loading from that moment on, and stops them on every future page. It cannot reach back and un-collect what was already collected during a visit when you had allowed it. To have earlier data deleted, ask us — see Your rights.

8. Browser-level controls

Independently of anything here, every browser lets you block and delete cookies, usually under Settings → Privacy. Safari, Chrome, Firefox and Edge all publish instructions, and all now block third-party cookies to some degree by default.

Blocking cookies entirely will not break this site. The only thing you lose is the memory of your consent choice, so the banner will ask again on each visit.

Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track

Both are honored, automatically. Some browsers and extensions send a Global Privacy Control ("GPC") or Do Not Track signal. This site reads either as a refusal of everything optional:

  • Analytics and advertising are switched off before anything loads — no PostHog, no Google tag, no Meta pixel, and no request to any of them.
  • You are not shown the cookie banner at all. You have already answered the question at browser level, and asking again would be asking you to repeat yourself.
  • The signal is read fresh on every page load and never stored. Switch it off and the site stops treating you as opted out; switch it on later and it takes effect at once.

If you would rather allow something here despite the signal, open Cookie settings. The panel tells you the signal is active and lets you tick a category anyway — that applies to this site only, and it is the one thing that overrides the signal.

GPC is a legally recognized opt-out in some places, notably California. Do Not Track carries no legal force and Safari has removed it, but somebody who switched it on meant the same thing, so it is treated the same way.

9. What about the app?

The Dailies app is not a web browser and sets no cookies. It does, however, include analytics of its own, and Apple's tracking prompt is part of that picture.

Its controls live in iOS rather than here: Settings → Apps → Dailies → Allow Tracking governs the advertising side. The full account — including what that switch does not cover — is in the Privacy Policy.

The two are entirely separate systems. Your choice on this website does not carry over to the app, and the app's does not carry over here.

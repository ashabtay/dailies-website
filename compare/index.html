If you have three seconds and your hands are full, the apps you press play on are the wrong shape however good they are. If you want ten proper minutes a day, a quote on a Lock Screen will not be enough. Plenty of people want both at different hours, which is why a lot of phones carry two of these.

Where Dailies sits

Dailies is a quotes app, so its nearest neighbors are Motivation and Daily Quote, with I am and Mantra close by. What it does differently is narrow: you build the feed out of topics, you choose how the words look, and it does not tell you when the next quote is coming.

Rather than one broad category, you follow topics, grouped under headings like Calm, Confidence, Humor, Work, Love and relationships, Faith, Spirituality, Self-kindness, Meaning and Direction, and specific underneath: when the worry won’t sit down, you’re allowed to be a beginner, make friends as an adult, say it while they’re here. Your feed is made only of the topics you follow, so a quote about something you did not ask for does not arrive at all.

The timing is the other half. You choose how many quotes you want, the hours you are happy to hear from it, and the days. Dailies picks the actual moments inside that window, so the quote lands as a small interruption in an ordinary afternoon rather than as the 9:00 alarm you learn to swipe away.

The library

The whole quote library ships inside the app, and a generous slice of it is free. Nothing has to load from a server and there is no account to make: every part of Dailies except the App Store purchase itself works with the phone in airplane mode.

Where the figures come from

Every figure about another app (its rating and rating count, prices, download size, languages, age rating and the devices it runs on) was read from that app’s US App Store listing, on 21 September 2026 for the first four comparisons and on 3 November 2026 for the rest, along with the feature list the developer publishes there. Feature claims come from the same listing and from its screenshots. Each page carries its own date because listings change; a figure with no date on it is one you cannot check.

Dailies is described from the shipping build, in words rather than figures. Its library, its free tier and its delivery limits move with every release, so a number here would go stale.

Each comparison runs the same ground: what the app sends you, how you steer what it sends, when it arrives, what you can change about how it looks, and what it costs.

App Store prices in this category are tested heavily, and several of these apps show different prices to different people on the same day. Treat every price here as a rough shape and check the listing before you buy.

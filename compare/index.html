Four shapes, not four brands

Every app on this page is filed under Health & Fitness and every one of them promises to make your day better. That similarity is skin deep. The useful question is not which app is best but what you want to be doing at the moment the app reaches you — and on that question they split cleanly into four.

AppWhat reaches youWhat you have to do
DailiesOne line of somebody else’s wordsRead it. Three seconds, on the Lock Screen.
MotivationOne quote, from a very large catalogRead it, or browse categories for more.
I amA sentence written in the first personSay it about yourself, and mean it.
ThinkUpYour own voice, recorded, over musicPut headphones on and listen.
MotivateVideo and audio from speakersSit down and watch for a few minutes.

If the honest answer is I have three seconds and my hands are full, the last two are the wrong shape no matter how good they are. If the honest answer is I want to work on this properly for ten minutes a day, a quote on a Lock Screen is never going to be enough. Most people want both at different hours, which is why a lot of phones have two of these installed.

Where Dailies sits

Dailies is a quotes app, so its nearest neighbors are Motivation and I am. What it does differently is narrow: you build the feed out of topics, and it does not tell you when the quote is coming.

Rather than one broad category, you follow topics — grouped under headings like Calm, Confidence, Humor, Work, Love and relationships, Faith, Parenting, Gratitude, Keep going, Self-kindness and Direction, and specific underneath: when the worry won't sit down, you're allowed to be a beginner, make friends as an adult, say it while they're here. Your feed is made only of the topics you follow, so a quote about something you did not ask for does not arrive at all.

The timing is the other half. You choose how many quotes you want, the hours you are happy to hear from us, and the days. Dailies picks the actual moments inside that window, so the quote lands as a small interruption in an ordinary afternoon rather than as the 9:00 alarm you learn to swipe away.

The library

The whole quote library ships inside the app, and a generous slice of it is free. There is no feed to load and no account to make: every part of Dailies except the App Store purchase itself works with the phone in airplane mode.

How we compare

Two rules, because a comparison page written by one of the apps in it is worth exactly as much as its sourcing.

  • Every number about another app comes from its own App Store listing, read on 8 September 2026 in the US storefront, and is dated on the page it appears on. Ratings counts, prices, sizes and languages all move; a figure with no date on it is a figure you cannot check.
  • No number about Dailies appears at all. The library, the free tier and the delivery limits all move with the app, so these pages describe them in words. A figure about us would be out of date by the time you read it; a figure about a rival is dated and sourced, which is what makes it checkable.

App Store prices in this category are tested heavily, and several of these apps show a different price to different people on the same day. Treat every price here as the shape of the thing, not a quote, and check the listing before you buy.

Where a rival does something better, these pages say so. Nobody has ever been persuaded by a comparison table where one column wins every row.

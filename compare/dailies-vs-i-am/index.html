Dailies vs I am

These two get filed together and they are not the same thing at all. I am gives you a sentence to say about yourself. Dailies gives you a line somebody else wrote, about the world. Which one helps depends entirely on which of those you can say without wincing.

The short answer

Choose I am if the practice you want is affirmation: reading — and meaning — first-person statements about who you are, several times a day, until the phrasing starts to stick. It is the biggest app in the world doing that, with 731,000 ratings and a 4.8 average, it runs on the Watch and the Mac as well as the phone, and it does it in fourteen languages.

Choose Dailies if I am confident and worthy of love makes you put the phone down. Dailies never puts words in your mouth. It hands you a sentence someone else wrote, aimed at the part of your life you said you were working on, and leaves you to take it or leave it.

This is the rare comparison where the deciding factor is a matter of temperament rather than features. If you already know which of those two paragraphs is you, the rest of this page is detail.

Affirmation or quote

An affirmation is a claim in the first person, in the present tense, written to be repeated: I am enough. I handle hard things. I am building something. The mechanic is repetition — you are meant to say it, often, whether or not you believe it yet.

A quote is somebody else’s sentence, and the mechanic is recognition. Nobody is asking you to claim it. You read a line about fear written by someone who was afraid two hundred years ago, and either it lands or it does not.

The practical difference shows up on a bad day. An affirmation on a bad day can read as an instruction you are failing to follow. A quote on a bad day is just company. Some people find the opposite — that a quote is passive and an affirmation is something they can actually do — and they are the readers I am is built for.

The overlap

Both apps let you write your own lines, so both can be bent into the other shape. In Dailies your own lines are kept under My quotes, join the rotation with the rest of the library on Premium, and stay on the device.

How each one decides what to show you

I am works from categories and intentions you pick — the areas you want to affirm, topped up with new material regularly. You choose the shelf and it draws from it.

Dailies asks the same question at much finer grain. Instead of a shelf, you follow topics: grouped under headings like Calm, Confidence, Humor, Work, Parenting, Faith and Keep going, but specific underneath — when the worry won’t sit down, you’re allowed to be a beginner, say the hard thing, push through the dull bit.

That granularity is the point. Two people can both pick Parenting and want opposite things from it: one wants parent in your own way, the other wants the days are long. A category alone cannot tell them apart, and Dailies does not make them share one. Your feed is built only from the topics you follow, so the lines that arrive are the ones you asked for — and you can change the set whenever you like.

When it reaches you

Both send reminders through the day and both have widgets, and for an affirmations practice the reminder is the product — I am’s own listing leads with setting reminders to arrive throughout the day.

Where Dailies differs is that it will not tell you when. You set how many quotes you want, which hours are fair game, and which days. Dailies picks the moments inside that window itself. The reasoning is simple: a notification at a time you chose becomes furniture, and one you did not choose stays a small interruption for much longer. Quotes also carry a time-of-day label, so a line written for the end of the day does not arrive at breakfast.

Dailies puts a quote on the Lock Screen as well as the Home Screen, and the Home Screen widget steps to the next line or shares the current one without opening the app.

What it costs

I am’s US listing showed $14.99 a month, $59.99 a year and $149.99 for life on 8 September 2026, alongside several other price points between $9.99 and $69.99 — the signature of a paywall that is being tested, so what you are offered may differ.

Dailies sells one thing: a yearly subscription with a free trial; the price in your currency and the length of the trial are shown on the purchase screen before you confirm. No monthly, no lifetime. It opens the full background, typeface and effect library, your own photos and colors behind the words, and the rest of the quote library.

App Store prices in this category change often and are frequently tested. Check the listing before you buy.

Where it runs

This one is not close. I am runs on iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, Mac, Vision Pro and inside iMessage. If you want an affirmation on your wrist, that is the app.

Dailies is iPhone and iPad, with a Home Screen widget and a Lock Screen widget, and no Watch app. What it gives up in surfaces it spends on the phone itself: the entire library is bundled in the app, so nothing loads, nothing buffers, and the whole thing works in airplane mode.

Questions

What is the difference between affirmations and quotes?

An affirmation is a first-person statement you say about yourself, meant to be repeated — I am capable of hard things. A quote is a line written by someone else that you read and either recognize or do not. I am is built on the first, Dailies on the second.

Does Dailies have affirmations?

Not as a category. Its library is quotes — original lines written for the app, plus public-domain writing. You can, though, write your own lines in any form you like, and on Premium they join the rotation with everything else.

Which is cheaper?

They are close on the yearly plan. I am listed $59.99 a year on 8 September 2026 and also sells monthly and lifetime; Dailies sells a single yearly subscription with a free trial and no other tiers. Prices in this category move, so check both listings.

Does either work offline?

Dailies does, completely: the library ships inside the app and reminders are scheduled on the phone, so everything except the App Store purchase works with no network.

Is there an Android version of Dailies?

Not yet. Dailies is iPhone and iPad today. I am is on both platforms.

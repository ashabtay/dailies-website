Dailies vs Innertune

Innertune is an audio app. It builds a playlist of affirmations for what you are dealing with, sets them over focus, relaxation or sleep beats, and plays them on repeat in a professional voice or your own. You press play and let it run. Dailies is the opposite shape: silent, one line long, and over in the time it takes to read it.

The short answer

Choose Innertune if you like the idea of affirmations playing while you work, walk or fall asleep. It is rated 4.8 across 12,000 ratings, it has a night mode for sleep, and its paid tier adds AI-personalized playlists, a choice of voices including ASMR, a library of beats and recording in your own voice.

Choose Dailies if you do not have headphones in, or do not want something in your ears all day. A quote reaches you as a notification and on a widget, on your Lock Screen, and you are done with it in seconds.

Listening or reading

Innertune’s idea is repetition: the same sentences, looped, until they become the background of your thinking. Audio is the natural medium for that, because it can run for an hour while you do something else, and recording the affirmations in your own voice makes them sound like your own thoughts. Its listing pitches it at anxiety and racing thoughts, sleep, self-doubt and negative patterns.

A quote on a Lock Screen does a smaller job. It does not run in the background; it interrupts for a moment and then gets out of the way. Dailies is designed for that moment: one line, sized to be read without unlocking, on a photograph and in a typeface you chose, from topics narrow enough to fit the week you are having.

Innertune is closer to ThinkUp than to any quotes app, and the same point holds: these are two different habits, and plenty of people keep one of each.

When it reaches you

Innertune sends daily reminders, and the content is in the app, waiting for you to press play.

In Dailies the notification is the content. You set how many quotes you want, the hours you are happy to hear from it and the days, and it chooses the moments inside that window itself. A Lock Screen widget and a Home Screen widget carry the same line in between. Nothing needs to be playing, and nothing needs headphones.

What it costs

On 3 November 2026 Innertune’s US listing showed Premium weekly at $4.99, monthly at $9.95 to $19.99, and yearly at $29.99, with further Premium prices between $29.99 and $69.99. Its listing counts zero ads among what Premium unlocks.

Dailies has a substantial free tier with no ads at any level. Dailies Premium is an optional subscription that opens every topic, the full background, typeface and effect library, your own photos and colors, and your Liked quotes and My quotes as feeds of their own. It is priced in your currency and shown in full before you confirm.

App Store prices in this category change often and are frequently tested. Check the listing before you buy.

Questions

Does Dailies have audio?

No. Dailies is text: one quote at a time, on a photograph you choose. Innertune is audio first, with affirmations played on a loop.

Can I use my own voice?

In Innertune, yes: its paid tier lets you record affirmations in your own voice. Dailies has no audio; you can write your own lines as text under My quotes.

Is Innertune for sleep?

Its listing includes a night mode for sleep and sleep beats. Dailies is meant for the waking day, with quotes inside the hours you choose.

Can I get quotes on my Lock Screen?

In Dailies, yes: there is a Lock Screen widget as well as a Home Screen one, and scheduled quotes arrive there as notifications through the day. Innertune’s listing does not describe a widget.

Which languages do they come in?

Innertune’s listing gives English. Dailies comes in English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German and Simplified Chinese.

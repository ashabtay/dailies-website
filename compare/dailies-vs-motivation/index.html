Dailies vs Motivation

The closest match on this site. Both hold a large library of quotes, sort them by topic, and send a few to your Lock Screen through the day. They part company on four things: how much you can change about how a quote looks, whether the app counts your days, who picks the minute it arrives, and what runs on your wrist.

The short answer

Choose Motivation if you want it on an Apple Watch, if you read in a language other than English or Spanish, or if a streak is the thing that keeps you coming back. It has 1.1 million ratings and a 4.8 average, and it is the safer download by a wide margin.

Choose Dailies if you care what the quote looks like and want real control over it, if you want the topics cut finer than a category, or if you would rather not be told when the next line is coming. It also has no ads at any tier and no sign-in.

They cost roughly the same, both have a free tier, and neither needs an account to start. Running both for a fortnight and deleting one is a reasonable way to decide.

What is the same

More than either side’s marketing suggests. Motivation’s listing describes thousands of quotes sorted by category, and Dailies’ library is in the same range rather than a fraction of it. Both keep working once installed, both send a handful through the day, and both carry the current line on a Home Screen and a Lock Screen widget. Both let you change the background, typeface and colors, favorite a line, share it as an image, and write your own. Both are free to install with a paid tier on top.

Making it yours

This is the clearest gap, and it runs in Dailies’ favour. Motivation offers themes, fonts and colors, and puts background photo customization behind premium. Dailies treats the look of the quote as the main thing you tune, and gives you a sheet with three tabs: background, a large set of photographs grouped by landscape — mountains, forest, sea, desert, night — alongside a handful of plain gradients; fonts, a wide range running from a book serif through a typewriter to a handwritten scrawl, with a size slider; and effects, a set of quiet motion overlays — drifting dust, fireflies, snow, mist, meteors, embers — painted over the photograph.

Everything previews live on a real quote as you tap, and nothing needs saving. A selection from each tab is free, chosen from across the range rather than clustered at one end, so a free install does not look like a demo. Premium opens the rest, along with your own photographs and your own solid colors behind the words.

Motivation wins one corner of this: its widget customization is extensive and it advertises thousands of combinations. But the effects have no equivalent on its listing at all, and in Dailies the styling follows the quote everywhere it appears.

Streaks and the Watch

Two things Motivation has that Dailies does not.

Streaks. Motivation counts consecutive days and shows you the run — its App Store screenshots lead with a one-week streak and a row of ticks. If a number going up is what gets you to open an app, that is a real feature and Dailies has nothing like it. Dailies does not count your days on purpose: the quote arrives whether or not you show up, and a broken streak is a reason to stop rather than to start.

Apple Watch. Motivation runs on iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, Apple TV and in iMessage. Dailies is iPhone and iPad. If you want a quote on your wrist, the comparison ends here.

And in the other direction, languages: Motivation ships in fourteen, Dailies in English and Spanish only — though very nearly the whole library is written out in Latin American Spanish rather than machine-passed.

When it reaches you

Both apps send quotes through the day, and both carry the current one on a widget. The difference is who picks the minute.

Motivation lets you set reminders through the day, and the number you can have is one of the things premium raises.

Dailies asks how many you want, which hours you are happy to hear from it, and which days — then picks the moments inside that window itself, without telling you in advance. A quote at 9:00 every morning becomes furniture within a week; a quote somewhere between 9:00 and 18:00 stays a small surprise much longer. Quotes carry a time-of-day label too, so a line written for the end of a day does not turn up over breakfast.

Every reminder is scheduled locally, so the whole app works in airplane mode.

What it costs, and the ads

Motivation is free to download with ads, and its listing shows ten in-app purchase price points between $3.99 and $47.99 — what a heavily tested paywall looks like from outside. Premium removes the ads and adds unlimited reminders, all themes and categories, and custom photo backgrounds.

Dailies has one product: a yearly subscription with a free trial, priced in your currency and shown in full before you confirm. No monthly, no lifetime. It opens the full background, typeface and effect library, your own photos and colors, and the rest of the quote library.

The structural difference is the free tier. Motivation’s is ad-supported, so removing ads is something you buy. Dailies carries no ads at any tier, which is also why its free tier is narrower: the free version is the trial, not the ad-funded edition.

On data, Motivation’s App Store privacy card lists purchases, user content, search history, identifiers and usage data as used to track you across other companies’ apps and websites. Dailies has no sign-in anywhere, favorites stay on the device, and the library ships inside the app, so reading a quote sends no request to anything. It does collect product analytics, written out in the privacy policy.

App Store prices in this category change often and are frequently tested. Check the listing before you buy.

Questions

Which has more quotes?

They are close. Motivation describes thousands of quotes by category, added to regularly, across fourteen languages — so it is the larger catalog once every language is counted, and the two are in the same range in English.

Does Motivation have streaks?

Yes. Its App Store screenshots show a streak counter with a row of days. Dailies has none and does not count your days at all.

Does Dailies have an Apple Watch app?

No. Dailies is iPhone and iPad, with a Home Screen widget and a Lock Screen widget. Motivation’s listing includes Apple Watch, Apple TV and iMessage.

Which one can I customize more?

Dailies, for the quote itself: a large set of photographic backgrounds, a wide range of typefaces with a size slider, and a set of quiet motion effects, plus your own photos and colors on Premium. Motivation offers themes, fonts and colors, with custom background photos on premium, and advertises extensive widget customization.

Is Dailies free?

Yes, with a paid tier and no ads at any tier. The free install includes a selection of backgrounds, typefaces and effects, the full delivery schedule and the topics setup picked for you. Premium is one yearly subscription with a free trial.

Do quotes still arrive with no signal?

In Dailies, yes. The library ships inside the app and reminders are scheduled on the phone, so everything except the App Store purchase works with no network at all.

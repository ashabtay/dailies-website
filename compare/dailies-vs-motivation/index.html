Dailies vs Motivation

Motivation is the biggest quotes app on the App Store and the closest thing Dailies has to a direct rival. They want the same slot in your day and go after it from opposite ends: one is built on breadth, the other on aim.

The short answer

Choose Motivation if you want the largest catalog in the category, you read in a language other than English or Spanish, or you like browsing — opening the app, moving through categories, and pulling out quotes to save and share. It has 1.1 million ratings and a 4.8 average for a reason, and no new app is going to out-catalog it.

Choose Dailies if you do not want to browse. Dailies is built for the reader who wants a few good lines to arrive by themselves, aimed at the thing they are actually working on, at moments they did not schedule. The library is smaller on purpose and the app has less to poke at.

They cost roughly the same and neither one needs an account, so having both for a fortnight and deleting one is a perfectly reasonable way to decide.

The library: breadth against curation

Motivation’s pitch is volume. Thousands of quotes, sorted into categories, topped up regularly, in fourteen languages. If you enjoy the act of looking through quotes — and plenty of people genuinely do — there is more here than you will ever get through.

Dailies ships a smaller library, and it is not going to grow into the tens of thousands, because most of it was written for this app rather than scraped. Every line is labeled with the topics it speaks to. The rest is public-domain writing and a few living authors’ lines used under their own published grants, and the license is stored per quote rather than assumed for the batch.

Neither approach is straightforwardly better. A larger catalog means more chances of the line that lands; a smaller, labeled one means fewer near-duplicates and less of the wallpaper-poster register that quote databases fill up with. What it does mean is that search matters to Motivation and does not exist in Dailies — if the thing you want is to look up a quote about patience at 11pm, Motivation is the app that does that, and premium there is what unlocks it.

How each one decides what to show you

Motivation asks you to filter. You pick categories and it draws from them, and you can go browsing whenever you want something else.

Dailies asks a short run of questions once, during setup — what you care about, what you want the words to do, how your days tend to go — and then does not ask again. It uses the answers to pick an opening set of topics for you from a long list filed under headings like Calm, Confidence, Humor, Work, Faith, Parenting, Gratitude, Keep going, Self-kindness and Direction. Every quote in the library is labeled with the topics it speaks to, and your feed is made only of the topics you follow.

That second layer is the one most quote apps do not have, and it is the one that does the work. Work on its own can mean a line about grinding harder or a line about not letting the job eat you, and those are opposite medicines; in Dailies they are different topics, and you follow the one you meant. Faith and Parenting are never opened unless you ask for them by name — a quote about your children, or about God, should not arrive because a similarity score said so.

Premium opens every topic to follow or drop as you like; a free install works within the set that setup chose. Either way the aim is yours rather than a category filter’s.

When it reaches you

Both apps put quotes on your Home Screen and both send reminders. The difference is who decides the minute.

Motivation lets you set reminders through the day, and the number you can have is one of the things premium raises.

Dailies asks for how many, which hours you are happy to hear from it, and which days. It then picks the actual moments inside that window itself, and does not tell you in advance. That is a deliberate design choice rather than a missing feature: a quote at 9:00 every morning becomes part of the furniture within a week, and a quote at some point between 9:00 and 18:00 stays a small surprise. Quotes also carry a time-of-day label, so a line written for the end of a day does not turn up over breakfast.

Dailies also ships a Lock Screen widget as well as a Home Screen one, and the Home Screen widget will step to the next quote or share the one you are reading without opening the app.

Worth knowing

Every reminder in Dailies is scheduled locally on the phone. Nothing is pushed from a server, which is why the whole app works in airplane mode — and also why turning notifications off does not break anything else.

Making it yours

Both apps let you change how a quote looks, and both make some of it paid. Motivation offers themes, fonts and colors, with background photo customization on premium.

Dailies has a large set of backgrounds, a wide range of typefaces and a set of quiet motion effects, with a few of each free. Premium adds the rest, plus your own photographs and your own colors behind the words. The free set is deliberately one from each corner of the library rather than several of the same thing, so a free install does not look like a demo.

Both let you keep favorites. Dailies additionally lets you write your own lines, which on Premium join the rotation alongside the library — they stay on the device and are never shown to anyone else.

What it costs

Motivation is free to download with ads, and its listing shows ten in-app purchase price points between $3.99 and $47.99 under names like Motivation Premium and Premium Motivation. That spread is what a heavily tested paywall looks like from the outside: which offer you see depends on where you meet it. Premium removes ads and adds unlimited reminders, search and custom photo backgrounds.

Dailies has one product: a yearly subscription with a free trial; the price in your currency and the length of the trial are shown on the purchase screen before you confirm. It opens the full background, typeface and effect library, your own photos and colors, and the rest of the quote library. There is no monthly plan and no lifetime tier, and there are no ads in the free version to remove.

App Store prices in this category change often and are frequently tested. Check the listing before you buy.

Ads, accounts and what leaves your phone

Dailies has no accounts and no sign-in anywhere in the app — not for reading, not for premium, not for keeping your favorites, which live on the device. Motivation’s listing does not mention a sign-in either way; if that matters to you, it is worth checking on first launch rather than taking anyone’s word for it.

The clearest difference is advertising. Motivation’s listing sells ad removal as a premium feature, which means the free tier carries ads. Dailies has no ads at any tier, and that is also why its free tier is smaller: the free version is the trial, not the ad-supported version.

Dailies’ whole library ships inside the app, so reading a quote involves no request to anything. What it does collect is product analytics — which screens are used, whether a purchase happened — and that is written out in full in the privacy policy, including the parts most policies leave implied.

Questions

Is Dailies free?

Yes, with a paid tier. A large slice of the quote library, a set of backgrounds and typefaces and the full delivery schedule are free, and there are no ads. Premium is one yearly subscription with a free trial, and it opens the rest of the library and the styling.

Does Motivation have a Lock Screen widget?

Its App Store listing describes a Home Screen widget. Dailies ships both a Home Screen and a Lock Screen widget. Listings change — check the current one if this is the deciding feature.

Which has more quotes?

Motivation, comfortably. It describes thousands of quotes added to regularly, across fourteen languages. Dailies ships a smaller library, in English and Spanish, and is not trying to win on volume.

Does Dailies need an account?

No. There is no sign-in anywhere in Dailies — not for reading, not for premium. Favorites and anything you write stay on the device.

Do quotes still arrive with no signal?

In Dailies, yes. The library ships inside the app and reminders are scheduled on the phone, so everything except the App Store purchase works with no network at all.

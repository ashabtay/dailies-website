Dailies vs Widgetsmith

Widgetsmith is the best known widget maker on the iPhone: photos, clocks, weather, countdowns, step counts and text, each one styled to match your wallpaper. Its listing pitches it for the words that mean the most to you, too, on the Home Screen or the Lock Screen. Dailies does only that last thing, but the line changes, and it comes from a library rather than from you.

The short answer

Choose Widgetsmith if you are designing a whole Home Screen. It is rated 4.6 across 779,000 ratings, it makes widgets for nearly anything (photos, calendars, weather, activity rings, time zones, countdowns) and it can swap what a widget shows by time of day. If one line you love is all you want, typed in once and left there, it does that well.

Choose Dailies if you want the line to change, and to be one you have not read before. Dailies fills its widgets from a large quote library, picked from the topics you follow, and sends the same quotes as notifications through the day.

They also sit together happily: Widgetsmith for the clock and the weather, Dailies for the quote.

Who writes the words

Widgetsmith’s listing talks about the people, places and words that mean the most to you, and about putting song lyrics on the Lock Screen. The words are yours: you type them, and they stay exactly as you typed them until you change them. For a line that already carries you, a verse or a name or a promise, that is exactly right.

A line you see forty times a day stops being read, though. Dailies is built for the opposite case: you choose the subject, it supplies the words. Follow topics as narrow as when the worry won’t sit down or say it while they’re here, and the widget keeps turning over to a new quote from them. If there is a line of your own you want in the mix, add it under My quotes and, with Premium, it comes around with the rest.

Home Screen and Lock Screen

Both apps put text on both screens. Widgetsmith can also place widgets in Control Center, and can change a widget’s content on a schedule, for example the weather in the morning and your calendar at work.

Dailies adds a surface Widgetsmith does not aim at: notifications. You set how many quotes you want, the hours you are happy to hear from it and the days, and it chooses the moments inside that window itself. The Lock Screen widget, the Home Screen widget and the notifications all carry quotes from the same feed. For the steps, see how to put quotes on your iPhone Lock Screen.

What it costs

Widgetsmith is free, with an optional Premium subscription, monthly or yearly. On 3 November 2026 its US listing showed Premium at $1.99, $3.99, $19.99 and $29.99; tide and weather data are Premium only.

Dailies has a substantial free tier with no ads at any level. Dailies Premium is an optional subscription that opens every topic, the full background, typeface and effect library, your own photos and colors, and your Liked quotes and My quotes as feeds of their own. It is priced in your currency and shown in full before you confirm.

App Store prices in this category change often and are frequently tested. Check the listing before you buy.

Questions

Can Widgetsmith show quotes?

It shows text you type in, so it can show any quote you already have. Its listing does not describe a quote library, so new quotes have to come from you.

Does the Dailies widget change by itself?

Yes. It turns over to new quotes from the topics you follow, and the same quotes arrive as notifications inside the hours you set.

Can I use both?

Yes. They do different jobs: Widgetsmith for photos, weather and the clock, and a Dailies widget for the quote.

Can I put my own words in Dailies?

Yes. Write them under My quotes, and with Premium they come back around in the feed, in notifications and on the widgets, mixed with quotes from your topics.

Which languages do they come in?

Widgetsmith’s listing gives English. Dailies comes in English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Simplified Chinese and Japanese.

Dailies vs Motivate

Motivate is a streaming library of motivational video and audio — speeches, interviews, playlists, five new videos a day. Dailies is one line of text on a Lock Screen. They are both sold as motivation and they behave like completely different products, because one of them needs your attention and the other one does not.

The short answer

Choose Motivate if what actually moves you is a speaker — a voice, a build, a swell of music, three minutes before the gym. It is well made, rated 4.8 across 8,300 ratings, it adds new videos daily, and it sorts everything into playlists across categories like Success, Fitness, Wealth, Wellbeing and Wisdom. Nothing text-based reproduces what a good speech does to you at 6am in a car park.

Choose Dailies if the thing you need has to survive contact with an ordinary Tuesday. Dailies never asks for minutes, headphones, a sign-in or a data connection. It puts a line where your eyes already are and gets out of the way.

They also sit at opposite ends of the price range in this category — Motivate’s annual plan was $95.99 on 8 September 2026, roughly three to four times what a quotes app charges.

Watching against glancing

A motivational video is a performance. It works by building — a voice, a rhythm, music underneath — and building takes time. You cannot skim it, you cannot half-watch it in a lift with someone standing next to you, and it does not survive being paused at ninety seconds. When you give it the attention it needs, it is far more powerful than a sentence. That is the trade Motivate makes, and for its audience it is clearly the right one.

A quote is the opposite trade. It carries less, and it costs nothing to receive. It works in a corridor, in a queue, in the eight seconds between putting your bag down and the next thing. Dailies is designed entirely around that eight seconds: one line, sized to be read on a Lock Screen without unlocking, on a photograph you chose, in a typeface you chose.

Which is to say the real question is not quality, it is occasion. Motivate needs you to have decided to be motivated. Dailies is built for the moment you had not decided anything at all.

Accounts, downloads and what happens on a bad connection

Motivate’s listing describes account creation and sign-in, which is what a streaming library needs to keep your playlists and downloads across devices. It also offers offline downloads and background playback, so a commute without signal is a solved problem — as long as you remembered to download something.

Dailies takes the other approach: there is no account and no sign-in anywhere in the app, and the entire quote library ships inside the download. Nothing streams, nothing buffers, nothing needs remembering in advance. Every part of the app except the App Store purchase itself works with the phone in airplane mode, including the notifications, which are scheduled locally rather than pushed from a server.

There is a size consequence too, in Dailies’ favour: text is small, and video is not.

When it reaches you

Motivate sends customizable push notifications and has a Home Screen quote widget, so it can nudge you. But the nudge is an invitation — the content is inside the app, and you have to go in and watch.

In Dailies the notification is the whole thing. You set how many quotes you want, the hours you are willing to hear from it and the days; it picks the moments inside that window itself and does not tell you when. If you never open the app, you have still had what you signed up for. A Lock Screen widget and a Home Screen widget carry the same line between notifications, and the Home Screen one steps to the next quote or shares the current one without opening anything.

What it costs

Motivate’s US listing on 8 September 2026 showed Pro at $11.99 a month, $95.99 a year and $399.99 for life. That is priced like a media subscription, which is fair enough: it is paying for produced video and a daily refresh of it.

Dailies sells one yearly subscription with a free trial; the price in your currency and the length of the trial are shown on the purchase screen before you confirm. It opens the full background, typeface and effect library, your own photos and colors behind the words, and the rest of the quote library. A large free slice of the library and the whole delivery schedule are free, and there are no ads at any tier.

App Store prices in this category change often and are frequently tested. Check the listing before you buy.

Questions

Does Dailies have videos?

No. Dailies is text — one quote at a time, on a photograph you choose. There is no video and no audio anywhere in it.

Do I have to create an account?

Not for Dailies — there is no sign-in anywhere in the app. Motivate’s listing describes account creation and sign-in.

Which works better without a connection?

Dailies works fully offline with nothing to prepare: the library is inside the app and reminders are scheduled on the phone. Motivate supports offline playback, but you have to download the videos first.

Which is cheaper?

Dailies, substantially. Motivate listed $95.99 a year on 8 September 2026; Dailies is one yearly subscription in the range typical of quote apps, with a free trial and a substantial free tier.

Can I get quotes on my Lock Screen?

In Dailies, yes — there is a Lock Screen widget as well as a Home Screen one, and scheduled quotes arrive there as notifications through the day.

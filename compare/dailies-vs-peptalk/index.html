Dailies vs PepTalk

PepTalk calls itself a pocket-sized motivational companion, and most of what is in the pocket is video and audio: speeches, talks and soundtracks for the gym, sorted into topics like Fitness, Wealth and Overcoming challenges, with quotes alongside. Dailies is text, one line at a time, delivered to the Lock Screen. They answer the same wish in two different amounts of time.

The short answer

Choose PepTalk if a voice is what gets you moving: a speech in your headphones on the walk to work, a talk before a workout. It is rated 4.9 across 45,000 ratings, it covers video, audio and quotes in one app, and its paid tier adds a daily alarm to wake up to and offline listening.

Choose Dailies if you need something that reaches you in the middle of a day you did not plan around motivation. It asks for no minutes and no headphones: the line arrives as a notification and sits on a widget, on your Lock Screen.

Pressing play or glancing

A pep talk works the way a coach’s voice works: it builds. Music underneath, a rising line, a story that lands at minute three. That needs you to press play and stay, and when you do, it can carry more than any single sentence. PepTalk is built for those minutes, and its listing leans into them: the walk, the warm-up, the commute.

A quote is the opposite trade. It carries less and asks nothing. Dailies is designed around the few seconds between one thing and the next: one line, sized to be read on the Lock Screen without unlocking, on a photograph you chose, in a typeface you chose.

So the question is less which is better and more when you need it. PepTalk serves the moment you decided to get fired up. Dailies serves the moment you had not decided anything.

When it reaches you

PepTalk’s paid tier lists daily reminders to start your day with positivity and a daily alarm to wake up inspired. Both point you into the app, where the content waits to be played.

In Dailies the notification is the whole thing. You set how many quotes you want, the hours you are happy to hear from it and the days, and it picks the moments inside that window itself. Between notifications, a Lock Screen widget and a Home Screen widget carry the same line.

Tone and content

PepTalk’s listing aims high and hard: overcoming temptations, crushing fitness challenges, defeating your inner demons. Its latest version adds an explicit content filter you can turn on in settings, which tells you some of the speeches use strong language. It is rated 13+ on the App Store.

Dailies has a wider range of registers. Some topics are about drive and work, many are quieter: calm, self-kindness, love, faith, meaning, a little humor. Every quote is checked before it goes into the library, and you choose which topics reach you, so the tone is the one you picked.

What it costs

PepTalk’s listing gives its Elite plan as $8.99 a month, or $53.99 a year with a 3-day free trial. On 3 November 2026 the in-app purchases on its US listing ranged from $5.99 to $59.99, so different people are likely being shown different prices.

Dailies has a substantial free tier with no ads at any level. Dailies Premium is an optional subscription that opens every topic, the full background, typeface and effect library, your own photos and colors, and your Liked quotes and My quotes as feeds of their own. It is priced in your currency and shown in full before you confirm.

App Store prices in this category change often and are frequently tested. Check the listing before you buy.

Questions

Does Dailies have videos or audio?

No. Dailies is text: one quote at a time, on a photograph you choose. PepTalk is mostly video and audio, with quotes alongside.

Which is better for the Lock Screen?

Dailies. It has a Lock Screen widget as well as a Home Screen one, and scheduled quotes arrive there as notifications through the day. PepTalk’s listing does not describe a widget.

Which works without a connection?

Dailies works fully offline with nothing to prepare: the library is inside the app and reminders are scheduled on the iPhone. PepTalk lists offline listening as part of its paid tier.

What do they cost?

PepTalk’s listing gives $53.99 a year. Dailies has a free tier with no ads, and Dailies Premium is priced in your currency and shown in full before you confirm.

Does PepTalk have explicit content?

Its latest version adds an explicit content filter you can turn on in settings, and it is rated 13+. Dailies checks every quote before it goes into the library.

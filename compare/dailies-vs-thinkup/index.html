Dailies vs ThinkUp

ThinkUp asks you to record affirmations in your own voice and listen to them, daily, over music. Dailies asks you for nothing at all after setup, and puts one line in front of you at a moment you did not pick. The gap between them is a gap in effort, and effort is the whole question.

The short answer

Choose ThinkUp if you want a practice and you will actually do it. Recording your own affirmations and listening back is a real, deliberate exercise — it has a session, it takes minutes, it wants headphones, and its users rate it 4.7 across 7,500 ratings. It also logs mindful minutes to Apple Health, which tells you what kind of app it thinks it is.

Choose Dailies if you know yourself well enough to know you will not sit down for it. Dailies has no session. It asks a few questions once, then a few good lines arrive during the day and you read one in three seconds while queueing for coffee.

The failure mode of ThinkUp is a recording you made in January and stopped playing in February. The failure mode of Dailies is a notification you swipe away without reading. Pick the failure you are less likely to have.

The effort question

Almost every difference below follows from one decision each app made about what it is allowed to ask of you.

Neither ask is wrong. A practice that takes five minutes and is done properly will do more than a line you half-read. The catch is the word properly, and most people know from experience which side of it they land on.

Your own voice, and why ThinkUp is built around it

ThinkUp’s central idea is that an affirmation in your own recorded voice carries further than one you read on a screen — its listing puts it as recording in the voice your brain trusts. Whatever you make of the claim, the app commits to it completely: the recording flow, the background music, the timer and the Watch app all exist to serve listening.

That commitment is also the cost. You have to record. Free accounts are limited in how many affirmations they hold and how long each recording can run, with those limits and the music library sitting behind premium. And once recorded, your library is as good as the twenty minutes you spent making it — which is fine when you are motivated and thin when you are not.

Dailies has no recording and no audio. Its equivalent move is the opposite: a library already written, most of them written for the app, so there is nothing to fill in. What you supply instead is a set of topics — the parts of your life you want to hear about — and the library sorts itself around them.

Where they meet

Dailies does let you write your own lines, and on Premium they join the rotation with the rest of the library. They stay on the device, they are never shown to anyone else, and there is no microphone anywhere in the app.

When it reaches you

ThinkUp notifies you to listen — morning, noon and evening, in its own framing. The notification is a prompt to start a session.

In Dailies the notification is the session. You choose how many quotes you want, the hours you are happy to hear from it and the days; Dailies picks the exact moments inside that window and does not tell you in advance. The line is on the Lock Screen when you look, and if you never open the app, you still got the thing you came for.

Both put something on your Home Screen. Dailies also has a Lock Screen widget, and its Home Screen widget will step to the next quote or share the one you are reading without opening the app.

What it costs

ThinkUp’s US listing on 8 September 2026 showed $7.99 a month, $39.99 a year and $99.99 for life, with a three-month option at $14.99. Premium is what lifts the caps on how many affirmations you can record and how long they run, and what opens the music.

Dailies sells one yearly subscription with a free trial; the price in your currency and the length of the trial are shown on the purchase screen before you confirm. It opens the full background, typeface and effect library, your own photos and colors, and the rest of the quote library. There is no monthly plan and no lifetime tier.

App Store prices in this category change often and are frequently tested. Check the listing before you buy.

Or both, honestly

These two do not fight over the same minute of the day, which is why plenty of phones could carry both. ThinkUp is a morning or bedtime thing, headphones on, ten minutes. Dailies is the other sixteen hours — the quiet nudge at 15:40 on a Tuesday when the morning session has long worn off. If you are already a ThinkUp user and it is working, the question is not whether to switch. It is whether the middle of your day is covered.

Questions

Can I record my own voice in Dailies?

No. Dailies has no audio and no microphone access at all. You can write your own lines, which on Premium join the rotation with the rest of the library and stay on your device.

Which takes less time each day?

Dailies, by a wide margin. A quote is a three-second read on the Lock Screen. ThinkUp is a listening session, and the app is designed around you giving it a few minutes.

Does either need headphones?

ThinkUp is much better with them, since the point is listening to your own recorded voice over music. Dailies is silent.

Which is cheaper?

ThinkUp listed $39.99 a year on 8 September 2026, and also sells monthly and lifetime. Dailies sells one yearly subscription with a free trial. Prices in this category move — check both listings.

Does Dailies need an account?

No. There is no sign-in anywhere in Dailies. Your favorites, your settings and anything you write stay on the device.

The short version

The distinction that matters

Some of it stays on your phone. The name you asked to be called, any photo you chose as a background, and anything you type are written to private storage on your own device. There is no account and no server holding them.

Most of how you have set the app up is also sent to analytics. Which topics you follow, your reminder frequency and hours, your chosen background, typeface and effect, and the identifier of any quote you heart are recorded against a random install identifier, so we can see how the app is actually used. The website is measured too, and carries advertising pixels once you allow them.

  • No account. You never make one, so there is no email address or password on file.
  • Your name and your photos stay on the device. Only the length of your name is ever sent, to tell a real answer from a keyboard mash.
  • You can switch the app's analytics off — one switch under Profile → Additional Info — and the website asks before it measures anything. The app itself does no advertising tracking and shows no tracking prompt.

Your own quotes and hearted quotes go to your iCloud account

Two things are mirrored off the device: the quotes you write yourself, and which quotes you have hearted. They are copied into your own iCloud account using Apple's CloudKit, so that a lost or replaced phone does not destroy the only copy of something you wrote.

To be clear about whose account that is

It is yours, not ours. The data sits in your personal iCloud storage under your Apple Account, governed by Apple's privacy policy. We cannot read it, we are not sent a copy, and it is never used for analytics or advertising. Turn iCloud off for Dailies in Settings → your name → iCloud and the app keeps working from the copy on your device.

Nothing else is mirrored. Your name, the topics you follow and your chosen look stay on the device, and a background photo you picked stays in your own photo library rather than being copied into your iCloud storage.

Some of these are also written to a shared app group container. That is still private storage on your phone; it exists only so the Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets can read the same quote and settings the app is using.

3. What the app measures

Dailies includes PostHog, an analytics tool, to record how the app is used. This is the part of the app that does send something, so it is worth setting out precisely.

What is recorded

  • Events — that a screen was opened, that setup was finished, that reminder settings were changed, that a quote was shared. The action, not its content.
  • A random install identifier — generated on your device to link one session to the next. It is not your Apple ID, your phone number or your email, and it is reset if you reinstall.
  • Technical context — device model, iOS version, app version, language, and an approximate location (country or region) derived from your IP address.

When it starts, and how to switch it off

Nothing is recorded on a fresh install until you press Get started on the welcome screen, under a line that points to this policy. Before that press the analytics tool is not even set up: no identifier is generated and no request leaves the phone.

To switch it off afterwards, open Profile → Additional Info and turn off Share usage analytics. From that moment nothing more is sent from your install. Turn it back on and it resumes. Nothing in the app is withheld either way.

Deleting the app stops the analytics, immediately and for good. If you also want the data already recorded to be removed, email us at help@mydailies.app and we will delete it.

4. Tracking and advertising

The app does not track you across other companies' apps and websites, and does not ask to. It shows no App Tracking Transparency prompt, reads no advertising identifier, and contains no advertising or attribution software from Meta, Google, TikTok or anyone else. Nothing in the app connects your install to an advert.

The one place advertising measurement exists is this website, and only behind the cookie banner — see section 5. If the app ever gains ad attribution, iOS will ask you first with Apple's tracking prompt, and this policy will say so before that version reaches you.

5. The website

mydailies.app carries analytics and advertising tags, and none of them load until you allow them. On your first visit you are asked; until you answer, the only thing stored is your answer itself.

  • Analytics — PostHog, with session recording switched off. Which pages are read, and where people give up.
  • Advertising — Google, Meta and TikTok pixels, which measure which advert led to a download and let those platforms show you ads elsewhere.

The two are separate switches, "Reject all" sits beside "Accept all", and you can change the decision at any time from Cookie settings in the footer of every page.

Every cookie, what it does and how long it lasts is listed in the Cookie Policy.

9. When you share something yourself

Each of these you start deliberately. None happens in the background.

  • Sharing a quote. The picture goes straight from your phone to WhatsApp, Instagram, Messages or wherever you chose. It does not pass through us, and we have no record of what you shared or with whom. Once it arrives, that company's policy governs it.
  • Saving a picture to Photos. Written to your own library. Whether it syncs anywhere is your iCloud settings, not Dailies.
  • Emailing support. Tapping Help or Suggest a feature opens your mail app. If you send it, we receive your email address and whatever you wrote, and use it only to answer you.
  • Rating the app. Apple's own dialog. Your review goes to Apple; We only see what is published publicly.

10. Apple's role

Because Dailies is distributed through the App Store, Apple collects some information under Apple's own privacy policy, separately from anything here:

  • App Analytics. If you agreed to share analytics with developers in your iOS settings, Apple shows us aggregated, anonymous figures — installs, rough geography, crash counts. Counts and charts; We cannot drill down to a person.
  • Crash reports. Only if you opted in. They describe the state of the program when it failed, not your quotes or profile.
  • Purchases. If you subscribe to Dailies Premium, Apple handles the whole transaction through StoreKit. We receive confirmation that a purchase happened and periodic, aggregated sales reports. We never see your card number, billing address or Apple Account details. Whether your subscription is active is stored on your own device so the app knows what to unlock; it is not held anywhere by us.

11. Children

Dailies is a general-audience app and is not directed at children. It is not intended for anyone under 18, and it does not knowingly collect personal information from them.

If you believe a child has provided personal information, write to help@mydailies.app and we will delete it.

13. How long anything is kept

  • On your device: until you delete the app, and not a moment longer.
  • Analytics events: kept for as long as they remain useful for understanding how Dailies is used. They are not deleted on a fixed schedule; what actually governs how long they survive is the retention setting on the analytics project itself. You can ask us to delete the events tied to your install at any time — see Your rights.
  • Advertising data held by Meta, Google and TikTok: under their own retention schedules, not ours.
  • Support emails: kept in our mailbox. We do not routinely clear out correspondence, so assume a message stays until you ask us to remove it — which you can do at any time, and we will.
  • Purchase records: as long as tax law requires, typically six or seven years.

14. Security

There is no account, no password and no server of our own, so there is no login to compromise and no database of ours to breach. What reduces the risk here is how little is collected and how few places it goes, rather than any claim to perfect defenses.

Everything that does leave your phone goes over encrypted connections to established providers, and is held in their infrastructure under their own security programs. On your phone, your settings, your name and any photo you chose sit inside the iOS application sandbox, protected by your device passcode and Apple's file encryption.

No system is perfect and we cannot promise absolute security. Practically, the most useful things you can do are the ordinary ones: use a passcode, keep iOS up to date, and be careful who you hand an unlocked phone to.
